# Original civilization base game

`src/simulation/BaseGame.luau` is an original, deterministic, service-independent civilization game. It uses the original `BaseRules`, indexed `WorldMap` and exact-schema `BaseSave` modules. This is synthetic balance and original orchestration under MPL-2.0; it does not implement or claim full Unciv parity. Legacy `Game`, `Rules` and version-1 `unciv-luau-original` saves remain separate and unchanged.

## Public API

The optional [whole-sphere mode](sphere.md) uses `mapShape="Sphere", frequency=1..22`, graph distance and complete globe coverage. Sphere state/snapshots omit planar coordinates and radius; deterministic public geometry is regenerated from frequency. Sphere saves use explicit version 2; all hex behavior and version-1 saves described below remain supported.

```luau
local BaseGame = require("./src/simulation/BaseGame")
local state = BaseGame.new(42, {humanPlayers=2, radius=4, difficulty="Normal", maxTurns=120})
local view = BaseGame.snapshot(state, 1)
local result = BaseGame.apply(state, 1, {kind="FoundCity", unitId=1})
local save = BaseGame.serialize(state)
local restored, errorMessage = BaseGame.restore(save)
local rules = BaseGame.getRules()
local preview, reason = BaseGame.previewAttack(state, 1, unitId, tileId)
local move, why = BaseGame.previewMove(state, 1, unitId, tileId)
```

`new(seed?, options?)` validates finite safe integer seeds, radius 3â€“40, `humanPlayers` 1 or 2, difficulty `Easy`/`Normal`/`Hard`, and a round limit of 20â€“500 (default 120). The default radius is 4 and difficulty is Normal. [Unciv-scale presets](map-sizes.md) expose Tiny (10) through Huge (40); pass the chosen preset radius. Existing small games and version-1 saves remain supported. `forest` is accepted as a legacy option and saved; new base maps use [coherent terrain and resource regions](world-generation.md), including forest and passable Tundra. Starting sites are selected from connected passable land with useful food/production and neighboring deployment space. A solo game starts one human with a settler, warrior and worker against an AI capital. Two-human games give both civilizations settlers, warriors and workers.

Two culture options shape the culture race. `cultureVictoryPolicies` (integer 1–6, default 6) is the number of adopted policies that wins by culture; a host's match preset can lower it (for example 4 for a quick match). `policyCostIncrease` (integer percent 0–400, default 50) raises each policy's cost by that percentage of its base cost for every policy the civilization already holds: cost = floor(base × (100 + increase × adopted) / 100). With the default the six policies cost 20, 37, 60, 100, 165 and 227 culture when adopted in rule order (609 in total, previously a flat 235); `policyCostIncrease=0` restores flat costs. **Default change:** new games use the 50% escalation, because the systems plan measured every flat-cost match ending by culture near round 40 regardless of map size or the round limit. Both values are stored in the game state, apply to the human and the AI, and are echoed in the snapshot.

`apply(state, actorId, command)` is the authoritative mutation boundary. The actor must own the active human turn; ownership, prerequisites, resources, funds, movement and exact command fields are checked. Rejected commands return `{ok=false,message=...}` without changing state or revision. Accepted commands increment revision once. A human EndTurn runs that civilization's economy; in solo games it also runs the AI turn. Round count advances after civilization 2 completes its turn. Unit movement and healing refresh at the beginning of the owner's next turn (see [Combat and healing](#combat-and-healing)).

| Command | Exact fields beyond `kind` | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MoveUnit` | `unitId`, `tileId` | Move to an observable reachable destination along the cheapest path under the [movement rules](#movement-and-zone-of-control) (partial moves, zone of control, one-third road steps); visible hostile targets initiate combat during war. |
| `RangedAttack` | `unitId`, `tileId` | Archer/Catapult attacks a visible enemy within range, spends movement, receives no retaliation and cannot capture. |
| `FoundCity` | `unitId` | Consumes a moving settler at a free site at least three tiles from another city. |
| `FortifyUnit` | `unitId` | Military unit spends movement and gains +50% defence until it moves or attacks; it counts as resting, so it heals at the start of each following turn. |
| `UpgradeUnit` | `unitId` | Applies the rule's upgrade chain with required technology, strategic resource and gold. |
| `BuildImprovement` | `unitId`, `improvement` | Worker begins a compatible improvement on owned land; order completes after its rule's number of owner turns. Moving cancels the order. |
| `SetProduction`, `Purchase` | `cityId`, `item` | Select or buy an eligible unit/building; purchases require a free deployment tile for units. |
| `SetCityFocus` | `cityId`, `focus` | Balanced, Food, Production, Gold, Science or Culture; changes tile selection or converts some production into science/culture. |
| `Research` | `technology` | Starts an available technology after prerequisites; science accrues at EndTurn. Changing research resets its progress. |
| `AdoptPolicy` | `policy` | Spends culture on a policy after its prerequisites; effects apply to city yields, happiness or upkeep. |
| `DeclareWar`, `OfferPeace`, `AcceptPeace` | `targetId` | Changes bilateral relations; accepted peace blocks war for eight rounds. |
| `ProposeTrade` | `targetId`, `giveGold`, `receiveGold` | Creates a peaceful gold offer, without reserving funds; offer lasts three rounds. |
| `AcceptTrade`, `CancelTrade` | `tradeId` | Accept atomically rechecks both treasuries and conserves gold; either participant can cancel. |
| `EndTurn` | none | Advances economy, improvements, research, AI when applicable and victory evaluation. |

Snapshots are detached and actor-specific. Common legacy display fields remain (`tiles`, `units`, `cities`, `gold`, `science`, `research`, `technologies`, `availableProduction`, `availableResearch`, `legalMoves`, `events`, `phase`, `turn`, `revision`). New fields include `actorId`, `activePlayerId`, `canAct`, `culture`, `policies`, `availablePolicies`, `happiness`, `resources`, `income`, `availableImprovements`, `diplomacy`, `trades` and `victoryType`. `availableProduction` is the union of eligible choices across the actor's cities. Prefer `availableProductionByCity` and `availablePurchasesByCity`, keyed by string city ID, for city menus; purchase choices also check treasury and deployment space. Legal moves are empty while another actor owns the turn. `availablePolicies` entries carry the actor's current escalated `cost`. `cultureVictoryPolicies` and `policyCostIncrease` echo the game's culture rules (a legacy save reports 6 and 0). `progress` is a public, coarse record for both civilizations, `{cities, policies, era}`, where `era` is `1 + floor(technologies / 4)` (1–5); exact technologies, economy and score stay private during play.

`previewMove(state, actorId, unitId, tileId)` describes one move on demand: `{unitId, tileId, path, movesSpent, movesLeft, endsMovement, zoneOfControl}`, or `nil` plus a message. `path` runs from the unit's tile to the destination. `zoneOfControl` is true when the zone of control ended the final step. Every `legalMoves` destination that is not an attack target has a preview, and no other tile does. The function never mutates state and ignores turn ownership. It runs the same search `MoveUnit` applies, so the path and `movesLeft` equal the applied result. Snapshots carry only `legalMoves` and no per-destination move records. On a paved Huge map at the unit cap those records made up most of the snapshot (about 2.7 of 3.1 MB in the library's own measurement), so a host asks for a preview when the player hovers over or selects a destination.

`attackPreviews`, keyed by string unit ID like `legalMoves`, lists one preview per legal attack target of each owned unit (empty while the actor cannot act). `previewAttack(state, actorId, unitId, tileId)` answers the same record for a single target, or `nil` plus a message; it never mutates state, ignores turn ownership, and gives one generic message for hidden, empty, peaceful or out-of-range tiles. A preview is `{unitId, tileId, target ("Unit"|"City"), ranged, exact, hiddenDefense, dealt?, received?, dealtMin, dealtMax, receivedMin, receivedMax, attackerHp, defenderHp, attackerDefeated, defenderDefeated, captures}`; the three outcomes are `"Yes"`, `"No"` or `"Maybe"`. Combat is deterministic, so against a unit outside a city the preview is exact (`dealt`/`received` present, equal to the applied result). Previews call the same strike function as combat and use only facts visible to the actor: its unit and its own units next to the defender (flanking), the visible defender's kind/hp/fortification, a visible city's population/hp and visible terrain. Enemy city buildings are never visible, so a city target (or a unit inside an enemy city) reports a range spanning no defensive buildings to every defensive building (currently Walls, +8) with `hiddenDefense=true`; the applied result always lies inside that range, and the preview is identical whether or not the city actually has Walls.

`cityForecasts`, keyed by string city ID, covers only the actor's own cities: `{food, production, gold, science, culture, growthTurns?, productionTurns?, productionBlocked?}`. It replays the actor's next economy step without mutating the state: the actor's cities in id order, each first making the territory claim the economy makes (a population-3 city claims its unowned work-radius tiles; the forecast records the claims in a private overlay), then the same per-city yield function the economy uses (citizens on the best distinct owned or just-claimed tiles for the focus, earlier cities' citizens excluded, buildings, policies, the Science/Culture conversion) and the same happiness and AI-difficulty adjustments, with happiness computed as the step computes it. `growthTurns` is 1 exactly when the step grows the city and is omitted when the city cannot grow at this rate (food not positive and the store short, happiness negative, or maximum population). `productionTurns` is 1 exactly when the step completes the queue, except for the unit-cap case below. When the queue cannot complete now it is omitted and `productionBlocked` gives the reason. `"NotAvailable"` means the item is unavailable: a missing technology, a building already present, the global unit cap, or too few strategic resources. Resources include improved resources on tiles the step claims first, minus those used by units that earlier own cities complete in the same step. `"NoSpace"` means a unit has no free deployment tile (the city tile and its neighbours, as purchases also require), counting tiles taken by earlier own completions. These reasons read only the actor's own cities, units, technologies and territory, the tiles around its cities and the units it can see. Unseen rival units never change a forecast. The global unit cap counts every unit, so the forecast applies it in two fog-safe ways. It uses the current total, the same `#units >= cap` check `availableProductionByCity` already exposes. It also adds earlier own completions this step to the units the actor can see (its own and visible rival units), which makes the block certain when that count reaches the cap. If unseen rival units bring the true total within those earlier completions of the cap, a later city's unit can still be stopped although its forecast says 1. This is the one fog exception (the cap is 169 units). Otherwise forecast and step agree exactly whenever nothing changes in between. They can differ if the actor acts first (moves, purchases, queue or focus changes, new cities) or, in a two-human game, when the other civilization's turn runs first and claims tiles, captures a city or occupies a deployment tile. Turn counts above 1 assume the current rate and conditions hold.

Cities are named in founding order from an original list per civilization: Sunhaven, Brightwater, Oakridge… for civilization 1 and Ironcliff, Embermark, Duskwall… for civilization 2, then `City <id>` once a list runs out. Capture changes the owner, never the name. Saves are unaffected because names were already free text.

Unknown tiles expose geometry plus `explored=false, visible=false`. Explored tiles retain terrain/resource and natural yields. Current owner, improvement and road are supplied only while visible. Enemy units/cities appear only while visible; enemy city queues, food, production, buildings and focus are never supplied. Enemy economy, research and score remain private during play; their score/statistics entries are zero placeholders, not inferred scores. Once `phase=="Finished"` both actors receive both real scores and statistics.

Loss events are owned by the civilization that suffered the loss, so its projection always includes them whatever its current fog (other actors see them only through public or tile visibility as usual). Events gain optional fields `cause`, `byOwnerId` (the responsible civilization) and `unitKind`:

| Event | Owner | Fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `UnitLost` | the unit's owner | `unitId`, `unitKind`, `tileId` (where it died), `amount` (damage taken), `cause` `Combat` or `Ranged`, `byOwnerId`. Emitted for a defender killed by melee or ranged attack and for an attacker that dies attacking. |
| `CityLost` | the previous city owner | `tileId`, `cause="Captured"`, `byOwnerId`; emitted alongside the capturer's existing `Capture`. |
| `Eliminated` | the defeated civilization, `public=true` | `cause` `NoCities` (lost every city and settler), `NeverFounded` (lost its settlers without ever founding a city) or `CapitalsLost` (the other civilization controls both original capitals), `byOwnerId`; precedes the public `Victory`. |
| `Disband` | the unit's owner | Existing upkeep event, unchanged in kind and message, now also with `cause="Upkeep"` and `unitKind`. Upkeep removal emits no separate `UnitLost`. |

The event ring holds 64 events. When it overflows it evicts the oldest routine event first and keeps `UnitLost`, `CityLost`, `Eliminated` and `Victory`. Only a ring made entirely of those kinds drops its oldest loss, and the event just added is never evicted. So one long command, such as an EndTurn that runs the AI and both economies, cannot push a player's loss out before the host projects it. Event ids always increase, and gaps appear only in a full ring. A host should still project after every accepted command. Hidden research/production events and global log are filtered out. Shared games omit the procedural seed while Playing; a private host must choose an unpredictable seed and must never replicate authoritative state or saves to clients. Visibility extends two hexes around own units/cities and refreshes after every accepted mutation. Travel uses paths wholly inside the currently visible area, so legal destinations cannot reveal hidden terrain or blockers. Exploration advances by moving toward visible edges.

`serialize` validates before copying and emits `{format="unciv-luau-base",version=1,state=...}`. `restore` validates before copying and rejects legacy/foreign saves; a host preserving old slots should dispatch `unciv-luau-original` to legacy `Game.restore`. Saves retain active actor, both economies, explored tiles, worker orders, diplomacy, pending trades, IDs, events and victory state. Derived topology/neighbor caches are rebuilt from validated tiles and are not serialized. Generator starting IDs and diagnostics are also transient. Loading preserves every stored terrain/resource record. NewGame and Restart use the current generator: the same seed reproduces geography within a generator version, but geography can change across generator upgrades. Separate legacy Game generation remains unchanged. `getRules` returns a detached, typed rule table.

Save compatibility: the format name and versions (1 Hex, 2 Sphere) are unchanged. The movement rules add no state field. Unit `moves` may now be any multiple of 1/6 from 0 to the unit's maximum. Earlier libraries accepted whole and half moves only, and those remain valid. A value within 1e-6 of a move of a sixth is accepted, so a third an encoder shortened to six decimal places (0.333333) still loads. Both `BaseGame.restore` and `BaseSave.restore` snap every unit's `moves` to its exact sixth in the restored copy; the save itself is not modified. So 0.333333 restores as 1/3, and a negligible 1e-16 restores as exactly 0. Every earlier save restores unchanged and plays under the movement rules from its next action. A save holding a third or a sixth is rejected (not misread) by library versions before these rules, so a host must not roll its pinned library back past this point once such saves exist. The combat and healing rules add no state: a save written before them restores unchanged and plays under them from its next action, and saves written now remain readable by the library version immediately before these combat rules (the one that already validates the culture and event fields below), which then plays them under its older rules. `getRules().terrains[*].defense` (a flat +3) is replaced by `defensePercent`. New states add optional `cultureVictoryPolicies` and `policyCostIncrease`, and events may carry `cause`, `byOwnerId`, `unitKind` and the kinds `UnitLost`, `CityLost` and `Eliminated`; all are validated when present. Every earlier save still restores: without the culture fields it keeps the original flat costs and six-policy threshold, so a loaded match does not change rules mid-game. Saves that use those culture or event fields are rejected (not misread) by library versions from before them, so a host must not roll its pinned library back past that point once such saves exist.

## Functional systems and evidence

The map contains plains, grassland, hills, forest, desert, tundra, water and mountains, with six bonus/luxury/strategic resources. Water and mountains are impassable for the land-only roster. Stable coordinate and neighbor indexes support weighted Dijkstra movement; cached local rings support vision and city work areas. Movement follows the [rules below](#movement-and-zone-of-control): partial moves, the zone of control and one-third road steps. Occupied friendly tiles and peaceful foreign land block travel.

Workers build Farm, Mine, Pasture, Plantation, Camp and Road. Farm/Mine can develop bare compatible terrain; the other resource improvements require their listed resource. Improved luxury resources increase happiness; improved Iron/Horses enable resource-dependent units. Existing units reserve their resource requirement immediately, including units purchased during the same turn.

Each city works distinct owned nearby tiles, excluding city centers. Citizens compete for nearby tiles in stable city ID order and choose tiles by focus with stable ID tie breaks. City centers provide base yields. Borders start at radius one and expand to the configured work radius at population three. Net food subtracts per-citizen consumption: surplus grows population, a deficit depletes food and reduces population. Happiness includes city/population pressure, luxuries, buildings and policies; negative happiness slows surplus growth and severe unhappiness reduces production. Units and buildings consume maintenance. Insolvency retires maintained military units, then reduces science for any residual deficit. Gold is bounded at zero. There is no automatic research queue: humans choose their next technology.

Sixteen technologies form a prerequisite tree ending in Rocketry. Eleven land unit kinds include civilian, melee and ranged roles. Eleven buildings alter city yields, defense or happiness. Six policies form a culture progression. Production completes one item per city per owner turn and preserves excess production; completed buildings reset the queue to Warrior. Unit deployment requires a free passable tile outside foreign cities, and foreign territory is available only during war, matching movement restrictions. Purchases and purchase menus use that same deployment rule. If no eligible deployment tile is free, unit production waits without spending accumulated production. SpaceProject requires Rocketry.

Diplomacy is bilateral between the two civilizations. Peaceful gold offers recheck funds at acceptance, expire or can be cancelled; war clears outstanding offers. The deterministic AI researches, adopts policies, selects production, develops owned tiles, scouts observed territory, founds cities, heals weak military units, attacks visible enemies, assesses peace offers and accepts favorable funded gold trades. Difficulty changes AI production yield. AI target planning uses its own visible/discovered area.

Domination requires control of both original capitals, or elimination of the other civilization's cities and settlers. Science requires Rocketry and an owned completed SpaceProject. Culture requires the configured number of policies (six by default) at escalating cost. Score victory uses configured city/population/unit/technology/policy weights at the round limit; ties favor civilization 1. Combat is deterministic synthetic damage, including ranged attacks and city capture; the formulas follow.

## Movement and zone of control

Movement is spent within one turn and refreshes at the start of the owner's next turn. Hills and Forest cost 2, other passable land 1, and Water and Mountain stay impassable. A unit may enter only tiles it can see. It cannot enter a tile holding another unit or an enemy city, nor foreign land unless at war.

- **Partial moves.** A unit with any movement left (more than 0) may enter an adjacent allowed tile even when the step costs more than remains. That step ends its movement (`moves` becomes 0). A Warrior with one move can therefore enter Hills, and a Warrior with two moves can cross one Plains tile and then enter Hills.
- **Roads.** A step between two road tiles costs a third of a move whatever the terrain (`getRules().roadMovement` is `1/3`; it was 1). A step onto or off the road network costs the entered tile's terrain. A two-move unit makes six road steps. Roads anywhere count, including enemy roads during war.
- **Zone of control.** This follows Civ V's rule for which enemies exert a zone of control (combat units and cities) and for what it does. A military unit (strength above zero, ranged units and Scouts included) that steps from a tile next to an enemy military unit or enemy city into another tile next to that **same** unit or city spends all its remaining movement. Stepping away from the enemy costs only the terrain. So does stepping into contact from a tile that was not next to it, so a unit can always close in. It then stops when it steps along the front. Two different enemies, each next to only one end of a step, do not combine. Only enemies count, which means the other civilization while at war; at peace nothing exerts a zone of control. Civilians (Settler, Worker) exert none, as in Civ V. They also ignore it when they move. That immunity is a deliberate divergence: Civ V has no blanket exemption for civilian movers. Scouts have no exemption in this rule set.
- **Fog.** Only enemy units and cities the moving civilization can see exert a zone of control. A hidden enemy therefore never changes a legal move, a preview or an applied move, so moving cannot reveal it. This covers every case that matters for a single move: an enemy next to the unit's starting tile is always visible (units see two hexes), and every path tile is visible. The only enemy that can be hidden yet next to a path tile stands three hexes from all of the mover's units and cities. It is ignored for that move, and once a move brings it into sight it applies to the next step. Civ V itself applies hidden units' zones of control. This is a deliberate fog-safety difference. One consequence: one long move may pass an unseen enemy in a single command, while the same route split into shorter moves can stop earlier, because the first move reveals the enemy and the zone of control then applies to the rest of the route.
- **Pathing.** `MoveUnit`, `legalMoves`, `previewMove` and the AI all use one search. It is Dijkstra over the movement spent, with ties settled by the lowest tile ID. Costs are whole sixths, so it keeps one bucket per sixth spent, which makes it linear in the tiles it reaches. A step that ends movement arrives having spent everything, and arrival never decreases as the spent amount grows, so the search stays exact. The applied move takes the cheapest path, which keeps the most movement.
- **Exact arithmetic.** The engine counts movement in integer sixths of a move. A sixth holds both the third a road step costs and the halves older saves may store. A unit's `moves` stores the canonical value `sixths/6`, so after one road step a two-move unit holds `10/6`. Hosts should format `moves` for display, for example to one decimal place or as thirds.

The AI moves under the same rules and uses only what it can see.

## Combat and healing

Combat has no randomness. One strike computes an attack and a defence strength, then damage both ways:

- **Attack** = `max(1, strength + 3 × level) × (0.5 + hp/200) × (100 + flanking%)/100`, using `rangedStrength` for ranged units.
- **Flanking** adds 10% per *other* friendly military unit (strength above zero, ranged units included) next to the defending unit, capped at +30% (`flankingPercent`, `maxFlankingPercent`). It applies only to melee attacks on a defending unit: ranged attacks and attacks on an undefended city get none, and civilians, the attacker itself and the defender's units never count.
- **Unit defence** = `max(3, strength) × (100 + terrain% + fortified%)/100 × (0.5 + hp/200)`. Hills and Forest give +25% (`terrains[*].defensePercent`) and fortification +50% (`fortifyDefensePercent`). The two add into one multiplier (+75% fortified on Hills). The `0.5 + hp/200` factor is the attacker's own health scaling, so a wounded defender is weaker and also retaliates less. Mountain and Water stay impassable.
- **Undefended city defence** = `(14 + population) × (100 + terrain%)/100`; a city on Hills gets +25%. Fortification and health scaling do not apply to cities.
- **City buildings** (Walls, +8) add a flat amount after those multipliers, for a city and for a unit defending inside one. Damage therefore stays monotonic in the hidden building defence, which keeps the preview's range exact.
- **Damage**: dealt `= clamp(floor(30 × attack/defence + 0.5), 8, 65)`; received `= clamp(floor(25 × defence/attack + 0.5), 8, 65)`, or 0 for a ranged attack or a civilian defender.

At full health on open ground a Warrior attacking a Warrior deals 30 and takes 25, as before this rule set. On Hills or Forest the result is 24/31, fortified 20/38, fortified on Hills 17/44, against a half-health defender 40/19, and with one, two or three flankers 33/23, 36/21 and 39/19 (more flankers stay at 39/19).

**Healing.** At the start of its owner's turn, a unit that neither moved nor attacked during that owner's previous turn heals `healing.city` (20) in one of its owner's cities, `healing.friendly` (15) elsewhere in its owner's territory, and `healing.neutral` (10) on neutral or enemy land, never above its maximum. Being attacked does not stop a unit healing. Whether a unit rested is derived from saved fields, so no state was added: moving spends movement and attacking spends all of it, and both clear fortification. A unit rested if it still holds its full movement, is fortified, or is a worker on an improvement order. Healing runs before movement refreshes. Fortify has no separate heal bonus; it holds the defence bonus and keeps the unit resting turn after turn. The derivation has three simplifications. A unit that moves and then fortifies in the same turn counts as rested. A unit that is newly built or bought spends its movement and so does not heal on its next turn; an upgraded unit spends its movement too, but one that was fortified stays fortified and so still counts as resting and heals. A worker whose order completes does not heal on the following turn. Cities still regain 5 HP at each of their owner's economy steps. This replaces the earlier rule of +15 for any fortified unit and +5 for other units in friendly territory.

`tests/unit/base-game.luau` checks both actors' ownership/turns, fog projections and private events, detached views/rules, acceptance of every advertised legal move for each actor, terrain movement costs, timed worker development and strategic resource reservation, city focus and starvation, research/building gates, bilateral peace/trade, ranged combat, all four command-driven victories, malformed-command atomicity and deterministic save/resume across varied AI seeds/difficulties. `tests/unit/base-events.luau` checks loser-owned `UnitLost`/`CityLost`/`Eliminated` (all three causes) and `Disband` events outside the loser's sight, their save validation, 140 unit-combat previews against applied results (kills, survivals and attacker deaths), city ranges that contain the applied result and do not depend on hidden Walls, generic answers for hidden targets, final score reveal with coarse progress, escalating and configurable culture rules, AI policy costs and legacy-save flat costs. `tests/unit/base-combat.luau` pins the combat and healing rules: the terrain and fortification percentages and their sum, wounded defenders, flanking with 0 to 5 units including the cap (civilians, enemy units, units out of contact and ranged attacks never count), 180 previews equal to both the applied result and an independent statement of the formula, city terrain defence, unflanked cities and hidden Walls, healing in each location (neutral, enemy, friendly, city, moved, attacked, fortified, worker order, cap at maximum), the AI's fortify-and-heal, a pre-G2 version-1 save that restores unchanged and then fights and heals under the new rules, and deterministic resumed replays. `tests/unit/base-movement.luau` pins the movement rules.

- **Partial moves:** into Hills and Forest with one move, after a Plains step, with a third of a move, and for a worker.
- **Zone of control:** stops along a front and free approach from outside contact. A two-step approach ends in one search. Two enemies do not combine (the same-enemy rule). Enemy cities and ranged units exert it. Enemy and own civilians, peace and Scouts are covered. A hidden enemy changes no preview or legal move until it is seen.
- **Roads:** steps of exactly a third, on Hills and Forest too, onto and off the network, and six steps for two moves.
- **Random scenarios:** 36 of them, over terrain, roads, territory, enemies, cities, war and peace. Every legal non-attack destination's on-demand preview equals both the applied move and an independent reference search. Nearby non-destinations are rejected, and removing hidden enemies changes nothing.
- **Saves:** a pre-G3 save with whole and half moves restores unchanged and then moves in thirds. Shortened values (0.333333, 0.666667, 1.666667) load through both restore paths as exact sixths, and 1e-16 restores as 0, so that unit cannot fortify. The sixth-based validation is checked.
- **Determinism:** solo wars replay identically with and without saves between turns.
- **Scale:** a paved Huge sphere at the 169-unit cap. Snapshots stay well under 1.5 MB and 1.5 s (about 0.5 MB and 50 ms measured), and one scout's on-demand previews stay cheap.

Foundation tests separately check topology/pathfinding and bounded save corruption rejection. Legacy tests continue to exercise the preserved old game.

## Deliberate scope limits

This is a bounded two-civilization land game. Religion, espionage, naval/air units, multiplayer beyond two humans, city states, barbarians, wonders, culture borders beyond the stated city growth rule, resource leasing, per-turn trade payments, tactical line of sight, diplomacy personalities, and a complete Unciv ruleset are outside this version. Ranged attacks use hex distance and visibility without obstruction tracing. No copied Unciv art, media or full rules data is shipped. The host/UI and network identity checks remain private integration responsibilities.
