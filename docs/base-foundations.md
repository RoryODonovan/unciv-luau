# Original base foundations

`BaseRules`, `WorldMap`, and `BaseSave` are original MPL-2.0 systems for the bounded civilization base game. They use the separately attributed `HexMath` coordinate distance; their map generation, balance, paths, projections, and validation are not converted Unciv systems. No full Unciv gameplay parity is claimed.

## Rules

The deeply frozen rule tree defines eleven unit kinds, sixteen technologies, eleven buildings, eight world wonders (buildings with `wonder=true` and empire-effect fields, listed in `wonderOrder`), six policies, six resources, and six improvements. Unit and building maps expose production and purchase costs, maintenance, prerequisites, and yields. Resources distinguish bonus, strategic, and luxury benefits. City-combat values (base strength, strength per era, garrison share, strike range and strike percentage) and a per-unit `siegePercent` (the Catapult's +50% against cities) are part of the same tree, as are four terrain features (`features`, `featureOrder`: Forest, Jungle, Marsh and Oasis with their terrains, movement, defence percentage, roughness, yields and allowed improvements) and the river values `riverGold`, `riverFarmFood` and `riverAttackPercent`, the golden-age values (`goldenAge`), a score weight per wonder and the barbarian values (`barbarians`: the owner id 0, camp timing, caps and spacing, unit caps, hunting and leash radii, the camp reward, the experience cap, healing, difficulty percentages and the unit kinds per era). The numeric balance is synthetic and intended for small deterministic matches, with radius 3 through 40 (37 through 4,921 tiles), an unchanged total limit of 169 units and 24 cities, 20 through 500 rounds (120 by default), and population capped at 100 per city. Science victory requires Rocketry and SpaceProject; the engine owns evaluation of these rules.

## Indexed maps and movement

`WorldMap.generate(seed, radius)` returns `{radius, tiles, index, neighbors, startIds}`. The original [coherent world generator](world-generation.md) supplies terrain, compatible resources and separated starting IDs from normalized centers and the adjacency graph. IDs correspond to array indexes. Coordinates use the pinned HexMath skew hex convention; the six offsets are `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(1,1)` and their opposites. `fromTiles(tiles, radius)` builds fresh topology indexes around the supplied mutable tile array. Indexes and neighbors are derived runtime caches, excluded from saves.

`shortestPath(map, start, goal, cost?)` returns a path including both endpoints and its total cost, or two nil values when blocked. `reachable(map, start, budget, cost?)` returns minimum costs, including the starting tile at zero. The cost callback receives `(fromId, toId)`. Costs apply to entering a tile, must be positive finite numbers, and nil blocks passage. Default movement rejects water and mountains, charges two for hills and forests, the larger of terrain and feature movement on a featured tile, one elsewhere, and one on roads. The base game supplies its own cost callback, in which a step between two road tiles costs `BaseRules.roadMovement` (a third of a move) and a step across a river ends movement; neither depends only on the entered tile, so the per-tile default cannot express them. `riverBetween(map, a, b)` tells whether the edge between adjacent tiles carries a river, and `riverNeighbors(map, id)` lists the tiles across a river from `id`; a tile's optional `river` mask indexes its neighbours in ascending id order (`map.neighbors[id]`). Dijkstra uses a binary min-heap over at most 4,921 map nodes; equal costs resolve by ascending tile ID, preserving the former scan order. Deterministic generation creates coherent terrain regions and chooses fair starting sites in connected land, without a straight Plains spine. Water and mountains shape passable routes; Tundra is passable with natural food one. See [world generation](world-generation.md) for the center contract and preserved save behavior.

`visibility(map, centers, range)` returns ascending visible tile IDs. It is geometric range without line-of-sight occlusion. `projectTiles(map, discovered, visible)` returns detached presentation data: unknown tiles reveal only coordinates/ID and fog flags; discovered tiles additionally reveal terrain/resources, feature and river mask; currently visible tiles additionally reveal owner/improvement/road. Explored terrain/resources are static knowledge; it does not maintain historical ownership snapshots. The engine separately filters units, cities, events, legal commands, and private player information.

## Validation boundary

`BaseSave.valid(save)` checks the exact versioned state schema before recursive copying. `serialize(state)` validates then returns detached data; `restore(save)` returns detached state or nil and an error. Unknown fields, invalid references, unsupported versions, metatables, nonfinite numbers, unsupported values, cycles, shared table references, sparse arrays, and out-of-budget trees are rejected. The format is `unciv-luau-base`, version 1, distinct from the unchanged legacy simulation save. Tile arrays may contain at most 4,921 records, while units and cities keep independent limits of 169 and 24. Structural traversal is limited to 81,920 entries (65,536 before features and rivers) and ten levels, enough for every Huge tile field and both complete explored arrays without allowing unbounded inputs. Tiles may carry an optional `feature` (a known feature on one of its terrains, with a resource and improvement it allows) and an optional `river` mask (an integer 1–63 whose bits name existing edges, in ascending neighbour id order, between two passable tiles, set on both sides; hex neighbours come from the validated coordinates and sphere neighbours from the public topology for the validated frequency). Unit `moves` is a multiple of 1/6 (within 1e-6 of a move) from zero to the unit maximum; whole and half moves from earlier saves remain valid, and thirds come from road steps. `restore` snaps each unit's `moves` to its exact sixth in the returned copy, as `BaseGame.restore` (which calls it) does. Units may carry optional `xp` (integer 0 to `maxExperience`, never above 0 on a civilian) and `promotions` (distinct known ids open to the unit's class, with prerequisites held, no more than `level` or than the levels the experience has earned); `level` is 0 to the number of experience thresholds (6). Cities may carry optional `strikeTurn` (an integer from 1 to the current turn), and events may carry an optional `cityId` (an issued city id), the kind `CityStrike` and the cause `CityStrike`. Players may carry an optional `goldenAge` record `{progress, turnsLeft, ages}` (integers; a running age needs at least one age begun), and events an optional `wonder` (a known wonder id), the kinds `WonderBuilt`, `WonderLost`, `GoldenAgeStarted` and `GoldenAgeEnded` and the causes `Happiness` and `Wonder`. A world wonder may stand in at most one city, and no city may queue a completed wonder. The state may carry an optional `barbarians` record `{camps, nextCamp}`: at most 12 camps `{id, tileId, founded, lastSpawn}` with distinct issued ids and distinct passable tiles holding no city and no civilization's unit, and rounds `1 ≤ founded ≤ lastSpawn ≤ turn`. Only with that record may units carry the barbarian owner id 0 (at most 30 such units, military, with no work order, experience, promotions or level, and needing no technology), events carry `ownerId=0` (on `Attack` and `UnitLost`) or `byOwnerId=0` (on `UnitLost`), and events use the kind `CampCleared`. Each unit, camp and event entry is checked for shape before its fields are read, so a malformed entry is rejected, never raised as an error. Saves without them remain valid. Radius remains the serialized Hex size; no new required fields or version bump are introduced. Starting IDs and diagnostics are transient. Loading preserves stored geography exactly; NewGame and Restart use the current generator, whose same-seed terrain can change across generator upgrades. The separate legacy Game remains unchanged.

Tests cover deep immutability, map determinism and topology, weighted optimality against an independent Bellman-Ford oracle, unreachable maps, bounded map settings, and fog field disclosure. Save tests exercise detached data and corrupt trust-boundary inputs.
