# Coherent world generation

New `WorldMap.generate(seed, radius)` and `WorldMap.generateSphere(seed, frequency)` worlds use the original, service-independent `WorldGeneration` module. It receives stable tile centers and the existing adjacency graph, returns terrain/resource/feature/river tile records plus two starting IDs, and leaves coordinate identity and topology unchanged. This is original synthetic geography under MPL-2.0, without a claim of Unciv map-generation parity.

Hex centers use the normalized planar coordinates `x=(axialX-axialY/2)/radius`, `y=0`, `z=(sqrt(3)/2*axialY)/radius`. Sphere centers are the public topology's unit vectors. The generator uses seeded, smoothly interpolated Cartesian fields with domain warping to build continents and coastlines, independent ridge belts, latitude and moisture-driven biomes, inland water and regional resource patches. It creates coherent regions rather than independent per-tile terrain rolls. Longitude seams and poles need no special graph edges. Resource placement remains compatible with the existing development rules.

Both starting sites lie in the same passable land component, have adequate separation for founding cities, and provide neighboring deployment cells and at least one adjacent work tile that provides both food and production for the first citizen. `BaseGame.new` deploys its existing settler, warrior and worker roster at these sites; solo games replace the rival settler with the rival capital. Deployment preserves generated terrain and resources. It no longer overwrites a fixed start or cuts a straight Plains spine between static sites. Water and mountains remain impassable for the current land roster, so continental coastlines shape travel.

## Features and rivers

Woods are a terrain feature on their own terrain: a wet tile becomes Plains or Grassland with a Forest, or a Jungle in the hot belt, and a cold wet tile Tundra with a Forest. Low featureless coastal grassland in a separate seeded field becomes Marsh, and a desert tile where a spring field is high becomes an Oasis unless a neighbour already has one. Forest tiles take Deer or (when warm, not on Tundra) Spices; Jungle takes Spices; Marsh and Oasis take no resource. New worlds therefore hold no Forest terrain; the terrain remains valid for earlier saves. Starting repairs treat a requested terrain as bare (a feature and its resource are cleared with it), clear a feature that cannot keep a newly placed resource, and place the luxury repair as Spices in a Plains Forest. A capital site never keeps a feature.

Rivers are carved last, from the finished terrain and the continent elevation (previously computed and discarded; Hills add 0.05). A river vertex is a corner where three mutually adjacent tiles meet, and the edge between tiles a and b joins the two corners whose triples contain both. Corners whose three tiles are all passable land are river sources, taken highest first (summed elevation plus a small seeded jitter); a source must not touch a tile that an earlier river borders or neighbours. From a source the river walks to the strictly lowest neighbouring corner, never through a corner touching a mountain, and stops when it reaches a corner touching water (its mouth) or a corner of an earlier river (a tributary). A walk that reaches a local minimum first, or has fewer than three edges, is discarded. Each step marks the edge on both tiles. At most one river per 55 passable tiles is kept, so maps with fewer than 55 passable tiles have none. Strictly falling height makes every walk finite, and the fixed iteration order makes the result deterministic for a seed. The same code serves hex boards and the sphere, pentagons included, because it reads only the adjacency graph.

Rivers change no starting-region food or production, so the start guarantees and diagnostics are unaffected; diagnostics add `featureFraction`, `rivers` and `riverFraction`. A Coast/Ocean water split is a follow-up: for the land-only roster it would change no rule, and a renderer can derive coast from neighbours.

## Tundra and saved maps

The terrain roster adds Tundra with movement cost one, no defence bonus (defensePercent 0) and natural food one. Tundra has no resource placements (a cold wet Tundra may carry a Forest, still without a resource) and receives no new farming or resource-development rules. The existing Road order also permits Tundra, consistent with roads on passable land. Other unit, resource, improvement and economy balance remains unchanged.

`map.startIds` is transient generation metadata. `fromTiles` and `fromSphereTiles` rebuild topology around existing tile records without running generation or selecting new starts. States, actor snapshots and saves contain neither starting IDs nor generator diagnostics or center/neighbor geometry. Hex saves remain version 1 and Sphere saves remain version 2; no generator version or new required save fields are introduced.

Loading preserves every stored terrain and resource exactly, including maps generated by earlier releases. NewGame and Restart use the current generator and reproduce the same seed within that generator version. The same seed is not promised to recreate the same geography across generator upgrades (the features-and-rivers generator is such an upgrade); restarting an earlier BaseGame can therefore produce updated geography, while loading its save restores the recorded map. The separate original legacy `Game` generation and save behavior remain unchanged.

`tests/unit/world-game.luau` exercises six seeds for all six sizes on both shapes, validates connected separated sites, legal complete deployment, useful work tiles, exact IDs/coordinates, deterministic reconstruction, geometry-free fog/saves, founding and actual turns. It also runs solo AI rounds at every size and checks that loading stored tile records does not regenerate them. Existing pathfinding oracles, schema-corruption and capture/victory regressions remain part of the public gate.
